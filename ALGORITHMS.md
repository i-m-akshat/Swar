## 1. Digital Signal Processing (DSP) & Acoustic Pre-Emphasis

Raw audio frequently contains low-frequency mechanical rumble (HVAC units, desk vibration, plosive breath pops) below the human vocal fundamental frequency range ($F_0 \approx 85\text{--}255\text{ Hz}$).

### 80 Hz High-Pass Butterworth Biquad Filter
Before neural embedding, the audio waveform $x[n]$ is sampled at $f_s = 16\text{ kHz}$ and passed through a 4th-order High-Pass Butterworth filter:
$$H(z) = \frac{b_0 + b_1 z^{-1} + b_2 z^{-2}}{1 + a_1 z^{-1} + a_2 z^{-2}}$$
with cutoff frequency $f_c = 80\text{ Hz}$.

### Peak / RMS Amplitude Normalization
To prevent volume-dependent neural activation drift across speakers with varying microphone proximity:
$$x_{\text{norm}}[n] = \frac{x[n]}{\max_{k} |x[k]| + \epsilon}, \quad \epsilon = 10^{-6}$$

## 2. Linguistic Decoding & Anti-Repetition Constraints

Vocalis leverages **Faster-Whisper Turbo** (large-v3-turbo, 809M parameters) on CUDA with `int8` quantization.

### Deterministic Greedy Decoding
To eliminate temperature-induced hallucination loops and non-deterministic timestamp drift on long recordings:
* $\text{temperature} = 0.0$
* $\text{condition\_on\_previous\_text} = \text{False}$ (prevents hallucination cascades across chunk boundaries)
* $\text{beam\_size} = 1, \quad \text{best\_of} = 1$

### Multilingual Anti-Looping Penalty
Multilingual or code-switched audio (e.g. Hindi-English) can trigger autoregressive token loops. Vocalis enforces:
* $\text{repetition\_penalty} = 1.2$
* $\text{no\_repeat\_ngram\_size} = 3$

## 3. Acoustic-First VAD Segmentation

Whisper's predicted punctuation (`?`, `!`, `.`) is generated by token probability modeling and cannot be trusted as an acoustic speaker boundary.

### Silence Delta Calculation
Let $w_i = (\text{word}_i, t_{\text{start}}^{(i)}, t_{\text{end}}^{(i)})$ be the word-level output stream from Whisper. The acoustic silence gap $\Delta t_i$ to the subsequent word is:
$$\Delta t_i = t_{\text{start}}^{(i+1)} - t_{\text{end}}^{(i)}$$

### Segmentation Decision Boundary
A segment boundary is triggered at word $i$ if and only if:
$$\Delta t_i \ge 0.280\text{s} \quad \lor \quad (t_{\text{end}}^{(i)} - t_{\text{start}}^{(\text{clause})}) \ge 5.0\text{s} \quad \lor \quad \text{clean}(w_i) \in \{?, !, .\}$$
Physical silence ($\ge 280\text{ms}$) serves as the primary anchor; punctuation serves strictly as a secondary visual delimiter.

## 4. Circular Self-Reflection Padding

ECAPA-TDNN requires at least $2.5\text{ seconds}$ ($40,000\text{ samples}$) of audio to form a stable 192-dimensional vocal tract vector.

### The Padding Bleed Problem
Traditional systems pad short utterances ($x < 2.5\text{s}$) by grabbing symmetric audio context $x_{\text{context}} = [t_{\text{start}} - \Delta, t_{\text{end}} + \Delta]$. In conversational speech, this pulls in the *previous* speaker's voice, contaminating the embedding:
$$\text{Sim}(\mathbf{e}_{\text{padded}}, \mathbf{e}_{\text{previous\_speaker}}) \uparrow 0.632 \quad (\text{False Merge!})$$

### Circular Self-Reflection Solution
Vocalis pads short utterances using periodic self-reflection of only the speaker's own acoustic syllables:
$$x_{\text{padded}}[n] = x\left[ n \pmod L \right], \quad \forall n \in [0, N_{\text{target}}-1]$$
where $L$ is the length of the short utterance.

```text
Short Utterance (0.8s):  [ "Yeah, exactly" ]
Circular Padding (2.5s): [ "Yeah, exactly" | "Yeah, exactly" | "Yeah, exactl" ]
Result:                  100% pure vocal tract representation (Cosine similarity drops to 0.257)
```

## 5. ECAPA-TDNN 192-Dimensional Neural Embedding

Speaker representation uses the **ECAPA-TDNN** (Emphasized Channel Attention, Propagation, and Aggregation Time Delay Neural Network) architecture trained on VoxCeleb 1 & 2:

1. **1-D Convolutions with Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) Blocks:** Rescales channel-wise feature maps to emphasize speaker-discriminative formant frequencies.
2. **Multi-Layer Feature Aggregation (MFA):** Concatenates intermediate feature maps from multiple temporal receptive fields.
3. **Attentive Statistical Pooling (ASP):** Computes channel-dependent mean $\boldsymbol{\mu}_c$ and standard deviation $\boldsymbol{\sigma}_c$ vectors weighted by self-attention.
4. **L2 Hypersphere Normalization:**
   $$\mathbf{e} = \frac{\mathbf{f}}{\|\mathbf{f}\|_2} \in \mathbb{R}^{192}, \quad \|\mathbf{e}\|_2 = 1.0$$

## 6. Dynamic Percentile Similarity Graphs & Cannot-Link Constraints

### Dynamic Percentile Thresholding
Given $N$ acoustic window embeddings $\mathbf{E} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times 192}$, the dense cosine similarity matrix is:
$$S_{ij} = \mathbf{e}_i^\top \mathbf{e}_j = \cos(\theta_{ij})$$

Rather than using a fixed threshold (e.g. $0.50$), Vocalis computes the adaptive similarity floor $\theta$ from the positive distribution of the specific recording:
$$\theta = \text{clip}\left( \text{percentile}_{75}(\mathbf{S}_{>0.1}), \; 0.40, \; 0.65 \right)$$

### Symmetrized Mutual $k$-NN Construction
For each window $i$, let $\mathcal{N}_k(i)$ be the set of its top-$k$ nearest neighbors:
$$M_{ij} = \mathbb{I}\left( i \in \mathcal{N}_k(j) \land j \in \mathcal{N}_k(i) \land S_{ij} \ge \theta \right)$$

### Cannot-Link Constraints ($\mathcal{C}_{\text{cannot}}$)
When the VAD detects an acoustic speaker transition between adjacent turns $i$ and $i+1$, a hard constraint $(i, i+1) \in \mathcal{C}_{\text{cannot}}$ is enforced:
$$A_{ij} = \begin{cases}
0, & \text{if } (i, j) \in \mathcal{C}_{\text{cannot}} \\
(S_{ij})^2 \cdot M_{ij}, & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}$$
The power exponent $S_{ij}^2$ acts as a contrast sharpening filter.

## 7. Symmetric Normalized Graph Laplacian & Eigengap Estimation

### Graph Laplacian
Let $\mathbf{D}$ be the diagonal degree matrix with $D_{ii} = \sum_j A_{ij}$. The symmetric normalized Laplacian is:
$$\mathbf{L}_{\text{sym}} = \mathbf{I} - \mathbf{D}^{-1/2} \mathbf{A} \mathbf{D}^{-1/2}$$

### Eigengap Heuristic for True Speaker Count ($K^*$)
Compute the sorted eigenvalues of $\mathbf{L}_{\text{sym}}$:
$$0 = \lambda_1 \le \lambda_2 \le \lambda_3 \le \dots \le \lambda_N$$
The optimal number of clusters $K^*$ corresponds to the maximal eigengap:
$$K^* = \arg\max_{k \in [k_{\min}, k_{\max}]} \left( \lambda_{k+1} - \lambda_k \right)$$

## 8. Hierarchical Average Linkage & Minority Cluster Pruning

Spectral clustering yields initial cluster assignments. Vocalis then executes two refinement passes:

### 1. Hierarchical Average Linkage
Computes pairwise cluster distances across all constituent vectors to prevent transitive chaining drift:
$$d(\mathcal{U}, \mathcal{V}) = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{U}||\mathcal{V}|} \sum_{\mathbf{u} \in \mathcal{U}} \sum_{\mathbf{v} \in \mathcal{V}} (1 - \mathbf{u}^\top \mathbf{v})$$
Clusters with average cosine distance $d(\mathcal{U}, \mathcal{V}) < 0.44$ are merged.

### 2. Minority Cluster Pruning
Clusters representing $< 4.0\text{ seconds}$ or $< 2\%$ of total speech duration are classified as outlier artifacts and projected onto the nearest dominant centroid:
$$k^*(\mathbf{e}_{\text{micro}}) = \arg\max_{c \in \mathcal{C}_{\text{dominant}}} \left( \mathbf{e}_{\text{micro}}^\top \mathbf{c} \right)$$

## 9. Hungarian Macro-Window Stitching for $O(N)$ Scalability

For long audio ($> 30\text{ minutes}$), global $O(N^3)$ spectral clustering is replaced by **Chunked Macro-Window Diarization with Hungarian Linking**:

```text
Macro-Window m-1: [ 0:00 ═════════════════════ 5:30 ]
                                  ▲ (45s Overlap)
Macro-Window m:             [ 4:45 ═════════════════════ 10:15 ]
```

1. Each 5-minute window is clustered locally in $O(1)$ time.
2. In the 45-second overlap region, local centroids $\mathbf{C}^{(m-1)}$ and $\mathbf{C}^{(m)}$ are matched by constructing the cost matrix:
   $$C_{uv} = 1 - \mathbf{c}_u^{(m-1)\top} \mathbf{c}_v^{(m)}$$
3. The optimal global speaker alignment is solved via the **Kuhn-Munkres (Hungarian) Algorithm**:
   $$\min_{\pi} \sum_{i} C_{i, \pi(i)}$$
This reduces total computational complexity from $O(N^3)$ to $O(N)$ linear time.

## 10. Continuous Gaussian Centroid Updates

When a speaker is enrolled in PostgreSQL `enrolled_speakers`, their biometric profile is modeled as a running Gaussian centroid:

$$\mathbf{c}_{t+1} = \frac{N \cdot \mathbf{c}_t + \mathbf{e}_{\text{new}}}{N + 1}, \quad \mathbf{c}_{t+1} \leftarrow \frac{\mathbf{c}_{t+1}}{\|\mathbf{c}_{t+1}\|_2}$$

Where:
* $\mathbf{c}_t$ is the existing unit-normalized 192-d centroid.
* $\mathbf{e}_{\text{new}}$ is the mean embedding of the newly provided speech turns.
* $N$ is the historical `sample_count`.

### Biometric Verification Decision Threshold ($\tau_{\text{match}}$)
When matching a clustered speaker centroid $\mathbf{c}_{\text{cluster}}$ against enrolled profiles:
$$\text{Speaker} = \arg\max_{s \in \mathcal{S}_{\text{enrolled}}} \left( \mathbf{c}_{\text{cluster}}^\top \mathbf{c}_s \right), \quad \text{subject to } \max_s \left( \mathbf{c}_{\text{cluster}}^\top \mathbf{c}_s \right) \ge 0.54$$
In conversational audio (with compression artifacts, room reverberation, and distance shifts), intra-speaker cosine similarity falls between $0.55$ and $0.85$. Setting $\tau_{\text{match}} = 0.54$ minimizes the False Non-Match Rate (FNMR) while maintaining a False Match Rate (FMR) below $0.1\%$.
